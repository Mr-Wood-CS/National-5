# Entity Relationship Diagram Tasks

You have identified what each database needs to store. Now show **how the entities connect** by creating an entity relationship diagram (ERD) in **draw.io**.

!!! info "Your goal"

    Draw a diagram with two entities, their attributes, clearly labelled primary and foreign keys, and a one-to-many relationship.

## Writing your answers in your workbook

Complete both tasks in your workbook under the heading **Entity Relationship Diagram Tasks**. Use a separate subheading for each task.

For each task, include:

1. **Your diagram** — create it in **draw.io**, then export it as an image and insert it into your workbook under the correct task heading. Make sure all entity names, attributes and relationship labels are readable. Save the original `.drawio` file so you can edit it later.
2. **Your explanation** — write one or two sentences describing the relationship in both directions.
3. **Your key choices** — identify the primary keys and explain which attribute acts as the foreign key and why it is needed.
4. **Your assumptions** — note any decisions you made where the scenario does not give enough detail.

Use your [analysis answers](../../3.2-analysis/3.2.1-analysis-tasks.md) and [data dictionaries](../3.3.1-data-dictionairies/3.3.1.6-tasks.md) to keep your entity and attribute names consistent.

!!! tip "Drawing in draw.io"

    Draw a box for each entity and list its attributes inside. Label each primary key **PK** and each foreign key **FK**. Join the entities and mark the **one** and **many** ends clearly, using the notation from the [relationship examples](3.3.2-db-relationships.md).

## Task 1

A customer has found a car they like, but the sales assistant needs to know which showroom to contact. Your database design must link each car to its showroom.

Use the **used-car dealership** scenario from Analysis Task One. For this task, assume each car is located at one showroom at a time.

(a) Draw an ERD for the two entities you identified, including their attributes.

(b) Label the primary key in each entity and the foreign key needed to link them.

(c) Show the one-to-many relationship clearly.

(d) Explain the relationship in your workbook. Use these sentence starters if needed:

* “One showroom can …”
* “Each car is located …”

(e) Explain how the foreign key allows staff to find the correct showroom's address and phone number for a car.


!!! question "Think it through"

    A car moves to another showroom. Which attribute in its record would need to change? Explain your answer in your workbook.

## Task 2

The librarian wants to search by an author's surname and find their books. Your database design must connect books to the correct author.

Use the **Highlands Library** scenario from Analysis Task Two. For this task, assume each book has one author, and an author may have written several books.

(a) Draw an ERD for the two entities you identified, including their attributes.

(b) Label the primary key in each entity and the foreign key needed to link them.

(c) Show the one-to-many relationship clearly.

(d) Describe the relationship in both directions, using full sentences in your workbook.

(e) Explain why an author's surname would be a poor choice of primary key.


!!! question "Think it through"

    A new book is added, but its foreign key does not match any author record. Explain why this would cause a problem and how referential integrity helps prevent it.

## Check your work

Before moving on, check both workbook answers:

* Have I named both entities and included their attributes?
* Have I labelled a suitable primary key in each entity?
* Is the foreign key on the **many** side, linking to the primary key on the **one** side?
* Have I clearly marked both ends of the relationship?
* Does my written explanation agree with my diagram?
* Have I answered the follow-up questions and recorded my assumptions?
